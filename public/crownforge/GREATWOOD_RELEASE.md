# The Greatwood

Release marker: `20260905-greatwood3`.

The Crownlands now grow broad, dense forests rather than evenly scattered specimen trees. Warped habitat regions bring related trees together: mature pines, deep spruce, oak/beech canopy, and birch/ash woodland. Interior stands contain roughly 95% dominant-family trees, with more companions along their margins. Overlapping mature crowns, shared stand heights, irregular clearings, and small internal glades make the woods read as connected places.

Five tested seeds contain 4,643–5,777 individually harvestable trees, compared with roughly 2,400–2,650 previously. Generation has a hard 8,500-tree ceiling and bounded sampling. Each tree still provides 240 wood; trunk collision, worker gathering stations, construction perimeters, faction separation, and the harvestable strategic ridge retain their gameplay contracts.

Meadows have new detailed grass/clover materials, warmer seed-grass patches, and habitat-correlated standing grasses and flower drifts. Shaded woods receive moss, leaf litter, and pine-needle floor materials. Twelve existing animated ground plants retain their root contact, wind, worker/hoof bending, depth sorting, and building exclusions. Visible plant counts and caches remain bounded.

## Existing kingdoms

New kingdoms receive the denser layout. Loading an existing save preserves its resource positions, depleted trees, buildings, and clearings; it receives the new rendering and ground materials without repopulating harvested land. A save generated by the pre-release source, including a completed Timber Yard and a felled tree, was loaded by this version and compared exactly for resources and buildings.

## Artwork

Created with the built-in `image_gen` tool. Selected generated PNGs are copied unchanged; clipping and mip preparation happen in the normal Canvas renderer.

- [Conifer atlas](assets/crownforge-greatwood-conifers-v1.png): 1,230 × 1,278 RGBA; two mature pine and two spruce silhouettes. Actual alpha verification found 795,991 fully transparent pixels. Authored crop bounds retain the complete crowns and roots.
- [Ground material atlas](assets/crownforge-greatwood-materials-v1.png): 1,254 × 1,254 RGB; meadow turf, seed grasses, conifer floor, and leafy/mossy woodland floor.
- [Exact generation prompts and provenance](GREATWOOD_ART_PROMPTS.json).

Two rejected conifer generations depicted checkerboards in RGB. They are not included. The final selected atlas has a true alpha channel. Existing broadleaf, character, building, and music assets are retained.

The habitat design takes inspiration from Forestry and Land Scotland's [woodland types](https://forestryandland.gov.scot/media/5qyaeamo/appendix-6-woodland-types.pdf) and Scottish Forestry's [native woodlands overview](https://www.forestry.gov.scot/scotlands-native-woodlands). This is an authored fantasy landscape rather than an ecological simulation.

## Performance and verification

Dense terrain exposed repeated multi-second searches toward resources in disconnected kingdoms. A shared resource-footprint connectivity map now rejects those impossible grid routes before A* expands. It is invalidated when navigation changes. Precise building collision and the existing complete route search still handle connected destinations; planned walls that clear resources bypass the shortcut. Endpoint exceptions retain access to gathering perimeters and occupied starts.

Tree mipmaps and visual caching bound rendering work. Visible resource wrappers are reused while retaining live resource references. Felling a tree updates its floor contributions instead of stamping all the forest again.

- Greatwood regression: coherent habitats, actual RGBA format and source bounds, 87 comparisons with independent flood-fill reachability, one-probe rejection of a disconnected kingdom, gathering endpoint exceptions, incremental floor equivalence after felling, clearing cleanup, live render-cache references, and pre-release save compatibility.
- Livingwood regression: five deterministic seeds, all twelve silhouettes, continuous ridge, protected clearings, opening a path by felling trees, six-minute food/wood harvesting runs, and save/load.
- Meadow regressions: all 15 field/render cases passed, including bounded caches, actual wind/contact geometry and rendering order.
- Building regressions: 50 foot/mounted routes, 22 construction completions, 25 repairs, 12 recruitment exits, eight gate crossings, and precise placement.
- Gathering, Hearthkin economy/work/combat, and dawn visual checks passed.
- Smoothness regression: 50,610 geometry cases, 101 approach-selection scenarios, and 350 simulated seconds with three workers building a Timber Yard and delivering 552 wood. Simulation p99 was 9.0 ms; maximum was 58.6 ms. The initial dense-map version had a maximum of roughly 10 seconds before the connectivity fix.
- Browser review covered pine, spruce, broadleaf, meadow, and whole-kingdom views. Sustained pine rendering of 824 visible resources measured p95 2.5 ms; the simulation maximum was 21.2 ms over 11,326 samples. The actual game's input handler placed a Timber Yard in 9.9 ms, completed construction, and displayed workers chopping, carrying `Wood`, and depositing at the yard without errors.

Timings describe this machine and test scenario, not frame-rate guarantees for every device. The optional `?perf` on [the forest review scene](dev/forest-studio.html) exposes local measurements; the normal review page hides telemetry.

Extended actual-game observation: 215.5 seconds, 12,921 simulation/render samples, 312 wood delivered, simulation p99 0.8 ms and render p99 3.6 ms. No frame intervals over 100 ms and no browser errors were measured. Source/public parity, changed-script syntax checks, whitespace checks and the full site build passed; unrelated generated website outputs were restored.
